Four ways to build the card.

Same trade in all four, so the only thing changing is the organisation. Colours are the grader's current palette and can be swapped for any of these. Pick one, or pick pieces from a few.

Concept 01

The Exchange

Both sides in view at once, because a trade is a comparison and you should not have to scroll to make it. One box, split down the middle, grades sitting above the column they belong to. Nothing is inverted or cut off. Costs you long player names.

The Desk

Consolidation is the whole story. The two starters Charlie shipped out come back as one receiver who outscores either of them on his own, and Sean was flexing a WR4 before this. Charlie is not robbed: Chase Brown walks into a backfield split that has trended his way for three weeks, and the FAAB covers the bench spot this opens. The grades separate because a lineup only starts three receivers, and Sean's third one just got much harder to beat.

Concept 02

The Receipt

Kills the boxes inside boxes. One card, hairline rules, no nested panels at all, so there is no spacing to get wrong and no border to argue with. The grade sits on the manager's own row instead of in a panel underneath. Full player names fit.

Concept 04

The Dispatch

The archive view. One trade is one row: a headline, the exchange in a single line, two grades. Five deals on a screen instead of one and a half. Tapping a row opens whichever full card wins above. Probably the right answer for the Past tab regardless of what Current uses.

What each one fixes. 01 and 02 both kill the light/dark panel flip and the uneven spacing. 02 removes nested boxes entirely, which is where most of the border and padding problems came from. 03 changes what the page is about: the verdict and the write-up, with the roster detail on request. 04 is a feed row and pairs with any of the three. Say which one to build, or which pieces to combine, and it goes in for real.
